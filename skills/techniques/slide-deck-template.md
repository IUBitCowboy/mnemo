# Slide Deck Template

Use this template when generating a slide deck.

```markdown
# Lecture [N]: [Title]

## Slide 1: Title Slide
- **Topic:** [Topic]
- **Course:** [Course-specific branding]

## Slide 2: Learning Objectives
By the end of this lecture, you will be able to:
- [Objective 1]
- [Objective 2]
- [Objective 3]

## Slide [N]: [Content Title]
- **Key Point:** [Concise explanation]
- **Code Example:**
  ```[lang]
  # Minimal, relevant snippet
  ```
- **Speaker Note:** [What to emphasize here]

## Slide [N]: Historical Hook [optional, placed near relevant content]
- [2-3 sentences on the origin of today's key tool/concept and the problem it solved]
- Place the hook near the slide where the concept is introduced, not
  necessarily at Slide 2. If no natural historical hook exists, skip it.

## Slide [Last]: Key Takeaways + Cliffhanger
- [Summary Point 1]
- [Summary Point 2]
- **Next time:** [A problem today's tools can't yet solve — pose it as a question to build anticipation]
```

## Key Conventions

- **One Concept Per Slide** — do not combine multiple distinct ideas on a single
  slide (from Cognitive Load Management skill).
- **Historical Hook** (optional) — placed near the slide where the relevant
  concept is introduced, not locked to Slide 2. The muse proposes 3 candidate
  hooks (or none), presented neutrally with no recommended favorite; the
  instructor selects, rejects, or provides their own. See the Historical
  Hook skill and the muse's slide deck conventions.
- **Speaker Notes** on complex slides to guide the instructor's delivery.
- **Key Takeaways + Cliffhanger** as the closing slide — summarize and pose an
  unsolved problem to build anticipation.
- **Instructor Prompts** (questions that surface student reasoning) belong in
  speaker notes, not in student-visible slide content.
