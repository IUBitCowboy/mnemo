# Standard Blocker Protocol

Include this section verbatim in every team-based exercise:

> **If you are individually blocked for > 15 minutes** on an error you cannot solve:
> 1. Notify your team and pivot to a support task (documentation, testing, research).
> 2. Create an individual branch: `aar-[exerciseN]-<username>`
> 3. Create `aar/AAR-[exerciseN]-<username>.md` using the After Action Report template
>    (see the AAR Template technique — embed it in the exercise so students can copy it).
> 4. Open a PR titled `AAR [Exercise N]: <Brief Blocker Description>`, assign the
>    Instructor as Reviewer.
> 5. Submit this PR URL to [course LMS] as your on-time submission (worth [points]).
> 6. After the instructor provides a hotfix, apply it, meet the Definition of Done, and resubmit
>    the passing PR (worth [points]).

The Standard Blocker Protocol teaches students that blocking is a normal part of engineering — the
protocol is to document, pivot, and submit the blocker as evidence of process,
not to sit stuck. The After Action Report is itself a learning artifact.
