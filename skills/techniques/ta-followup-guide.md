# TA Follow-up Guide Structure

Every team-based exercise and project assignment must include a **TA Follow-up
Guide** (in an HTML comment block so it's instructor/TA-only, not student-visible):

```markdown
<!--
## TA Follow-up Guide

- **In-Class Goal:** [TA's #1 priority during the session]
- **Final Definition of Done (11:59 PM):** [What must be submitted]
- **Common Pitfalls:**
  1. **[Blocker]:** Symptom / Fix
  2. **[Blocker]:** Symptom / Fix
- **Coaching Questions:**
  - "[Question probing the why]"
  - "[Question probing team process]"
- **Feed-Forward Prompts:**
  - "[Prompt pointing toward the next topic]"
-->
```

TAs are **Team Coaches**. They encourage, share experience, and help teams
unblock. They have no authority beyond applying the provided rubrics.

The TA Follow-up Guide gives TAs:
1. A clear in-class priority (what to watch for during the exercise)
2. A clear end-of-day requirement (what must be submitted)
3. Anticipated pitfalls with symptoms and fixes (so TAs can diagnose quickly)
4. Coaching questions for the next team meeting (feed-forward, not just feedback)
5. Prompts that connect this exercise to the next topic (building continuity)

When generating the TA Follow-up Guide, propose **4 candidate coaching
questions** for the TA to use in the next team meeting. The instructor may:

- Select one or more of the 4 candidates
- Reject all 4 and ask you to try again
- Provide their own questions

Do not skip this step — the coaching questions are where the instructor
shapes the TA's pedagogical engagement with each team.
