# Instructor AI Use Symmetry

The disclosure standard imposed on students must be the same standard the
instructor holds themself to in work product produced for students. If students
must attach an AI use disclosure to their submissions, every AI-assisted
artifact the instructor produces for students must carry an equivalent
disclosure.

## Principle

A one-way transparency regime is enforceable but not credible. If the
instructor's assignment sheets, rubrics, lecture notes, and slide decks are
AI-assisted but undisclosed, the integrity policy asking students to disclose
their AI use lacks moral authority. Students who notice this asymmetry have a
legitimate grievance that undermines the entire policy.

The symmetry principle requires:

1. **Instructor disclosure on artifacts.** Every AI-assisted artifact visible to
   students carries a standard disclosure: what AI tool was used, what it
   produced, and what the instructor reviewed or changed.

2. **Stated instructor principles.** The instructor's AI use principles are
   visible to students — in the syllabus or a companion document. Students can
   see not just the rule but the reasoning behind it.

3. **Human accountability.** All material visible to students has been reviewed
   and approved by a human. If the material is wrong, the AI did not write it
   wrong — the instructor failed to review it correctly. Accountability rests
   with the human, not the tool.

## Muse Behavior

The muse does not enforce the instructor's principles — it does not know them
in detail and should not be loaded with instructor-specific policy. The muse's
responsibility is narrow and structural:

- Every generated artifact includes a standard disclosure field in its
  template. The field states that the artifact was generated with AI
  assistance through the Mnemo system and has been reviewed by the instructor.
- The disclosure field is a placeholder the instructor confirms or edits before
  publishing. The muse does not claim review has occurred — it flags that
  review is required.
- The disclosure does not enumerate specific AI uses (that level of detail
  lives in the instructor's principles document, not in each artifact).

## What the Muse Does Not Do

- The muse does not decide whether AI use is appropriate for a given artifact.
  That is an instructor decision.
- The muse does not evaluate whether the instructor has lived up to the
  principles. That is self-assessment, not muse behavior.
- The muse does not produce the instructor's principles document. That is
  authored by the instructor and lives in the knowledge base, not in the muse
  layer.

## When to Use

- Always. This is a baseline principle, not a situational technique.
- Applies to every course built on the Mnemo system, regardless of course
  content, level, or instructor.

**HIL awareness:** The disclosure field on generated artifacts is a structural
reminder, not a verification. The instructor must confirm that review has
occurred before publishing. Note in the HIL block whether the artifact's
disclosure field has been confirmed by the instructor.
