# Pedagogical Analysis

After generating any student-facing artifact (exercise, assignment, slide deck),
append a **Pedagogical Analysis** section. Group your analysis under the three
categories below. For each dimension that applies, write 1–2 sentences
explaining how it was applied or noting a concern.

## Knowledge & Cognitive

- **Core Goal ([course domain]):** Does this simulate a real [course domain]
  task, or is it a toy exercise?
- **Cognitive Load Management:** How does scaffolding (starter code, worked
  examples, phased structure) keep focus on the core objective? Note: this
  dimension is in productive tension with Productive Failure Design — high
  load is sometimes intentional and correct.

## Skills & Practice

- **Productive Failure Design:** Does the exercise create space for productive
  struggle before instruction? If cognitive load is intentionally high, note
  why the struggle is productive rather than overwhelming.

## Dispositions & Metacognitive

- **Metacognitive Reflection:** Does the exercise prompt students to plan,
  monitor, and self-evaluate their process? Include a self-check: did you
  (the muse) open with praise instead of substance?
- **Historical Context:** Does it ground the tool or practice in its origin
  story? Those ignorant of history are doomed to repeat it.
- **Ethical Challenge:** Does it surface an ethical dimension of the practice?

## Course-Specific Dimensions

Each muse's base file includes a `## PEDAGOGICAL ANALYSIS — COURSE DIMENSIONS`
section with additional dimensions specific to the course, grouped under the
same three categories. The Mnemo skill provides the format and standard
dimensions; the muse provides the course-specific ones.

## HIL Awareness

The Pedagogical Analysis section is generated as part of the artifact and should
be included in the galley copy with appropriate HIL markup. The instructor
reviews the analysis as part of the human review step — it is not student-facing
content.
