# Cognitive Apprenticeship

Structure learning as a progression: **modeling → coaching → scaffolding →
fading**. Early in the course, model expert behavior explicitly (code reading
walkthroughs, debug sessions, design decisions). As the course progresses,
fade: reduce scaffolding, increase autonomy.

## Evidence

Collins, Brown, and Holum (1989, *American Educator*) proposed cognitive
apprenticeship as an adaptation of traditional apprenticeship for cognitive
skills. The model has been extensively adopted in education research and is
particularly relevant to software engineering education where "expert practice"
is the goal.

The six methods of cognitive apprenticeship:

1. **Modeling** — expert makes their thinking visible (think-aloud problem
   solving, code walkthrough with rationale)
2. **Coaching** — observe students working, offer hints, feedback, reminders
3. **Scaffolding** — provide structure (starter code, templates, worked
   examples) that students can't yet produce themselves
4. **Articulation** — ask students to articulate their reasoning (why did you
   choose this approach?)
5. **Reflection** — have students compare their work to expert work
6. **Exploration** — push students to define their own problems and approaches

## The Fading Principle

The critical element is **fading** — the gradual withdrawal of support across
the semester. A course that scaffolds heavily in Week 1 should scaffold less
in Week 8 and minimally by Week 15.

When generating an artifact, note in the Pedagogical Analysis whether the
scaffolding level has changed from the prior week. If you reduced scaffolding,
state what was removed and why. If you maintained or increased it, state why
fading was not appropriate this week.

Rough fading trajectory:
- **Weeks 1–4:** Heavy modeling and scaffolding (starter code, worked
  examples, step-by-step instructions)
- **Weeks 5–10:** Coaching and articulation (reduce starter code, require
  students to explain their reasoning, role rotation in ICEXs)
- **Weeks 11–15:** Exploration (minimal scaffolding, students define their
  own approaches, project work)

## Relationship to Existing Skills

- **Cognitive Load Management** provides the scaffolding mechanism
- **Productive Failure** is what fading *enables* — once scaffolding is
  removed, students can productively fail
- **Abstraction Literacy** (in Clio) is a form of articulation — naming the
  principle makes the expert's reasoning visible

## When to Use

- Across the full semester — the apprenticeship arc IS the course structure
- Early weeks: heavy modeling and scaffolding
- Middle weeks: coaching and articulation (ICEXs with role rotation)
- Late weeks: exploration (project work with minimal scaffolding)
